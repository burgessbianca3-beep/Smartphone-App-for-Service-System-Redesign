# Smartphone-App-for-Service-System-Redesign

## Overview
This project focused on redesigning the service system at the University at Buffalo’s Moe’s Southwest Grill location by introducing a mobile ordering extension through the existing GET Mobile App. The purpose of the redesign was to reduce customer wait times, improve queue efficiency, and enhance the overall student experience during peak lunch and dinner hours.

The project combined queueing analysis, observational data collection, service system evaluation, and user testing to determine whether a mobile ordering system could improve operational performance.

---

## Problem Statement
The Moe’s Southwest Grill location inside the University at Buffalo Student Union experiences heavy congestion during peak hours. Long customer lines lead to:

- Increased wait times
- Queue overcrowding
- Balking (customers leaving before joining the line)
- Reneging (customers leaving after joining the line)
- Jockeying (switching lines)

Although the university’s GET Mobile App already supports ordering at several dining locations, Moe’s did not yet have mobile ordering capabilities.

The goal of this project was to redesign the current service system by integrating mobile ordering and creating a grab-and-go pickup process.

---

## Objectives
- Analyze the current service system at Moe’s Southwest Grill
- Measure customer arrival rates and service times
- Evaluate queue performance during lunch and dinner rushes
- Design a mobile ordering extension for the GET App
- Test usability and efficiency of the redesigned system
- Recommend improvements to reduce congestion and wait times

---

## Methodology

### Data Collection
The team collected observational data during the busiest operating periods:

- Lunch Rush: 12:00 PM – 2:00 PM
- Dinner Rush: 5:00 PM – 7:00 PM

Data was collected in 15-minute intervals and included:

- Number of customers in the system
- Number of customers waiting in queue
- Service times per order
- Number of servers available
- Order characteristics

### Queueing Analysis
Using the collected data, the team evaluated:

- Average number of customers in the system
- Average queue length
- Average wait time in the queue
- Average total system time
- Worker utilization ratios

### Prototype Development
A prototype extension of the GET Mobile App was designed to allow students to:

- Place mobile orders
- View current wait times
- Choose pickup options
- Retrieve orders from a designated pickup area

### User Testing
Volunteer UB students tested the app prototype by completing mock orders while their interaction times were recorded.

---

## Key Findings

### Service Times
- Average service times were similar during lunch and dinner periods
- Orders took approximately 1 minute and 45 seconds to complete

### Queue Performance

#### Lunch Rush
- Average people in system: 15
- Average people in queue: 10
- Average system wait time: ~52 minutes
- Average queue wait time: ~41 minutes

#### Dinner Rush
- Average people in system: 14
- Average people in queue: 9
- Average system wait time: ~36 minutes
- Average queue wait time: ~24 minutes

### Worker Utilization
The utilization ratios indicated that employees could potentially handle both in-person and mobile orders simultaneously without exceeding operational capacity.

### Prototype Testing
The app testing demonstrated that students could complete mobile orders quickly:

- Average total order completion time: ~48 seconds
- Students could rapidly decide whether to order online or wait in line based on displayed wait times

---

## Proposed Redesign
The redesigned service system included:

- Integration of Moe’s into the GET Mobile App
- A dedicated mobile order pickup area
- Separate queues for in-person and mobile orders
- A grab-and-go locker or pickup station

This redesign was intended to:

- Reduce physical line congestion
- Shorten average wait times
- Improve customer flow
- Increase convenience for students
- Improve restaurant throughput and revenue potential

---

## Results and Impact
The proposed redesign demonstrated several potential benefits:

- Improved operational efficiency
- Reduced customer frustration
- Better time management for students
- Increased flexibility in ordering
- More balanced queue distribution

The project showed how service system redesign and mobile technology integration can significantly improve customer experience in high-volume university dining environments.
